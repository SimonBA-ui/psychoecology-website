Psychoecology

An educational project about how digital technologies change human psychology - and how we can adapt to those changes consciously.

It shapes how we pay attention, what we expect, how we relate, and how we understand ourselves.

01

What is Psychoecology?

Psychoecology explores psychological difficulties and adaptation needs that emerge or intensify under modern digital technologies. It looks at how technology changes attention, habits, emotions, relationships, social norms and our sense of self.

The aim is not to make technology the enemy. It is to understand what is changing and develop practical ways to preserve more awareness, judgment and choice inside a digital environment.

The project combines public education, research, discussion, practical exercises and attention or meditation practices when they are useful to the subject.

A changing technological environment requires psychological adaptation, not only new devices and new rules.

Topics

Psychoecology is broader than digital addiction. These are some of the questions the project explores.

01

Attention and stimulation

What happens to sustained attention, boredom, reading and quiet when high stimulation is always available?

02

Digital habits

Why do some online behaviors repeat even when we no longer want them - and what makes change more realistic?

03

Beliefs and social norms

How do platforms, peers, advertising and new cultural expectations shape what feels normal or necessary?

04

Relationships and sense of self

How do online comparison, public identity, metrics and constant communication affect relationships and self-perception?

05

AI, thinking and work

How do AI assistants and automation change competence, authorship, dependence, learning and expectations of work?

06

Information and uncertainty

How do news, endless searching, repeated checking and information overload affect trust, anxiety and decision-making?

Psychoecology Club

The central live format is a public lecture-seminar: one topic, useful ideas, practice and discussion.

Public meetings

Lecture, practice, discussion

Each meeting is designed to be useful on its own while also giving people a reason to return and continue the conversation.

  • A focused lecture on one Psychoecology question
  • Selected research, books or current technological developments
  • A practical exercise or small experiment
  • Discussion of questions and concrete situations
  • A short attention or meditation practice when relevant

Starting small

First public cycle

The first cycle is deliberately small: four meetings before deciding on a permanent rhythm.

The goal is to learn which topics people want to continue, what format creates real discussion, and what can be sustained alongside ordinary work.

No membership platform or complicated community system is required to begin.

04

Working tools

Five recurring directions help structure Psychoecology discussions and practical work.

  1. 01

    Examine beliefs

    Ask what a technology or behavior appears to promise, and compare that promise with actual experience.

  2. 02

    Independent judgment

    Separate personal needs and judgments from assumptions reinforced by peers, platforms and digital culture.

  3. 03

    Strong attention

    Notice internal states, triggers and automatic reactions early enough for conscious choice to become possible.

  4. 04

    Needs and alternatives

    Identify the real need behind a recurring pattern and explore other practical ways to meet it.

  5. 05

    Learn from returns

    Treat a return to an old pattern as information: what happened, what changed, and what can be prepared differently next time?

05

Educational, not clinical

Psychoecology is an educational project. It does not provide medical care, psychotherapy, psychiatry, diagnosis or crisis support.

Discussion and exercises in group meetings are optional. Participants can decline personal questions or exercises, and attention or meditation practices are presented as educational practices rather than treatment.

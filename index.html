01

Is the internet taking up more of your life than you want?

Internet addiction. Digital addiction. There is no single formal medical diagnosis under those broad names. But compulsive use of digital services is still a real problem.

The screen promises a lot. But it often takes more than it gives. Psychoecology helps adults whose internet use and digital entertainment are getting out of control and interfering with work, sleep, relationships and the things that genuinely matter.

Scrolling, social media, news, video, games, pornography, conversations with AI or endless information searches can quietly consume an evening, a night or a workday. You already see what is happening. You install blockers. Delete apps. Promise yourself you'll stop.

Then, after a while, it starts again. This is not simply a lack of willpower. Some part of us believes the familiar digital behavior is doing a job: distracting us, calming us, stimulating us, helping us avoid or forget something unpleasant for a while.

Until the beliefs behind the behavior are examined in depth, prohibitions are ineffective.

Tools

  1. 01

    Rational examination of beliefs

    We examine the beliefs and illusions that keep compulsive behavior going.

  2. 02

    Independence of beliefs

    We reduce dependence on social pressure and develop a more independent view of digital habits.

  3. 03

    Strong attention

    We develop the ability to notice an automatic reaction before it determines behavior.

  4. 04

    Alternative behavior patterns

    We look for practical ways to meet the needs that previously led to the familiar digital behavior.

  5. 05

    Relapse prevention

    We identify high-risk situations, prepare responses before a strong urge appears, and analyze lapses so one episode is less likely to become a return to the old cycle.
